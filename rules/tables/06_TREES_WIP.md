## 1. The Six Main Role-Trees

The firmest part of the design. A main tree is identified by **the thing a character acts on to win**.

| Main tree | What it acts on | Strategy | Typical fantasy RPG classes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Assault** | **HP** — physical injury | **Quick win.** Remove a decisive opponent before they can contribute again. | Fighter, Assassin |
| **Defender** | **SP** — shock and overwhelm | **Attrition.** Sustain the contest until the opponent gives way. | Knight, Paladin |
| **Heavy** | **BP** — balance and displacement | **Break / wreck.** Break footing or position and exploit the opening. | Barbarian, Warrior |
| **Archer** | **Enemy movement** | **Distance and disruption.** Prevent, delay, or redirect movement by threatening an approach. | Archer, Hunter |
| **Skirmisher** | **Own movement and relative position** | **Maneuver and tempo.** Reach an objective, change engagements, or disengage in time to act elsewhere. | Rogue, Ranger |
| **Commander** | **Morale** | **Psychological advantage.** Sustain the company’s willingness to fight or influence the opponent’s. | Warlord, Bard |

**Archer and Skirmisher** illustrate why this works. At a crossing, the Archer wants the enemy to hesitate before crossing. The Skirmisher wants to get across while the opportunity lasts. Both can carry a bow and still have different reasons for choosing a hex.

**Placements:** **Control** is at the supportive level. **Magical healing** belongs in casting, and **nonmagical healing** in utility. **Berserker** remains a recognizable fantasy through Fury, Intimidation, and potentially a later corner. The main roster is six.

**Six main trees, three corners each: 18 main archetypes.**

**Assassin, Slayer, and Bodyguard** belong in secondary/support development. Provisional placements: §2, Supportive-Tree Corners.

## 2. The Eight Supportive Trees

A supportive tree defines **how a companion practises their role**. It can be aggressive: Fury and Ambush are both examples. **Cultural training appears here** through distinctive nodes and named archetypes (`99`, Character identity).

| Supportive tree | Tactical style | How it changes two different mains | Typical fantasy RPG archetype |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Duelling** | Commit to one opponent and exploit their openings. | **Assault** singles out a champion for a quick HP finish. **Defender** occupies a champion through sustained SP attrition. | Duelist, Aldori Defender |
| **Formation Fighting** | Coordinate position and movement with nearby companions. | **Defender** gives ground alongside a wounded ally. **Heavy** breaks an opponent’s balance as a companion advances into the gap. | Tower Shield Specialist, Standard Bearer |
| **Ambush** | Choose where and when first contact occurs. | **Archer** waits until the enemy crosses a ford before threatening its retreat. **Assault** lets the guards pass to engage the exposed commander. | Assassin, Knife Master |
| **Control** | Restrict an opponent’s next action or movement. | **Defender** holds a pursuer while an ally withdraws. **Skirmisher** throws the pursuer off balance and uses the opening to reach an objective. | **Monk** (grappler), Ranger (trapper) |
| **Momentum** | Generate force through movement or exploit an opponent’s incoming momentum. | **Heavy** uses a mounted charge or flying dive to break BP. **Defender** braces a pike to receive the charge. | Mounted Knight, Pikeman |
| **Beast Handling** | Give an animal a tactical task of its own. | **Archer** sends a hound after a fleeing enemy while covering a crossing. **Heavy** keeps the animal close to exploit a knockdown. | Hunter (Divine Hound), Barbarian (Mad Dog) |
| **Fury** | Sustain or inspire commitment as an engagement intensifies. | **Assault** presses for an HP finish despite mounting risk. **Commander** draws wavering companions into a renewed push. | Barbarian, Skald |
| **Intimidation** | Use fear to erode the opponent’s willingness to continue. | **Heavy** uses a knockdown to frighten those still standing. **Commander** pressures a wavering group into withdrawing. | Rogue (Thug), Dirge Bard |

**Mounted Combat is a confirmed corner of Momentum.** The lone pikeman receiving a charge also belongs under Momentum because the incoming movement is what makes the action valuable. A pike line covering adjacent approaches draws on **Formation Fighting**. Whether receiving a charge deserves its own Momentum corner is OPEN. Flight can change mobility across several mains, and a dive can use Momentum; a separate progression rule for flight is OPEN.

**Source of the last three:** the reverse survey of *Pathfinder: Wrath of the Righteous* added **Beast Handling, Fury, and Intimidation**. The clearest example was the Divine Hound and the Mad Dog: one sends the animal where the hunter cannot go; the other wants the animal fighting at their side. Other candidates explored — Focus, Counterfighting, Bodyguarding, Targeting — remain outside the eight. *(Counterfighter is now a Defender corner, §1; Bodyguard a support archetype, placement OPEN.)*

| Supportive tree | Corner 1 | Corner 2 | Corner 3 |

| **Ambush** (concealment and first contact) | **Assassin** — reaches a chosen opponent unseen for a decisive opening strike. | **Waywatcher** — watches an approach from concealment and chooses whom to let pass. | *Open* |
| **Control** (restricting an opponent’s actions) | **Disarmer** — denies an opponent the use of their weapon. | **Monk** — controls an opponent at close range through holds and releases. | **Bodyguard** — draws attention away from a companion and uses speed to evade attacks. |
| **Momentum** (movement into contact) | **Mounted Combat** — uses a mount’s speed and mass at contact. | **Pikeman** — turns an incoming charge against the charger. | *Open* |
| **Beast Handling** (cooperation with an animal) | **Handler** — directs the animal to carry out a task independently. | **Beast Fighter** — fights alongside the animal, each creating openings for the other. | **Scout** — uses the animal’s senses to locate and track opponents. |
| **Fury** (heightened aggression) | **Berserker** — sustains personal aggression through pain and pressure. | **Skald** — rouses companions to share that aggression. | *Open* |

**Culture** usually replaces an existing supportive corner through its cultural name and distinctive training nodes. In rare cases, a tradition has its own supportive tree. The six main trees and their corners are shared across cultures.

## 3. The Utility Trees (Working Roster)

A utility tree holds smaller, complementary jobs on the mission map. The player may bring a fighter partly because they can also treat a casualty, or operate a machine.

| Utility tree | Current directions | Concrete mission-map use |
|---|---|---|
| **Machine** | **Artillerist** is a confirmed corner. **Traps** belong here, with **Trapper** a proposed corner name. **Wright** is a candidate. | An Artillerist operates a ballista. A Trapper deals with a trap. A Wright restores a damaged gate mechanism or gangway while the rest of the company holds the position. |
| **Physic & Poison** *(working name)* | **Herbalist** (field medic), **Alchemist**, and **Poisoner** are the working directions. | The Herbalist tends a casualty and recognizes remedies. The Alchemist adapts a preparation to the situation. The Poisoner considers which target and timing make a scarce preparation worth using. |

## 4. The Three Casting Trees

**Each conduit is one way of using wyrd and therefore gets its own progression tree**, with active and passive abilities. A caster can develop a conduit as their primary investment, or a fighting style can be complemented by casting. The conduits are owned by `../01_INTERNAL_ARCHITECTURE_WIP.md` Spirit Layer §2.

| Conduit | Attribute (Óðr pillar) | Method | Acts on |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Galdr** | **WIL — The Decree** | Ordering | **Rules** — bans and limits |
| **Rúnar** | **INT — Resonance** | Tuning | **Objects and hexes** — the map |
| **Seiðr** | **CHA — The Shadow** | Negotiating | **Actors and spirits** |

The same purpose can call for different kinds of casting. To keep an escape passage open, a Galdr user might limit pursuit, a Rúnar user might tune the gate, and a Seiðr user might negotiate with its guardian. These are illustrative; corners and individual abilities are OPEN.

## 5. Saga and Historical Examples

Found by the reverse method: first identify **a person doing a recognizable job**, then ask where that capability belongs.

| Historical or saga example | Design result |
|---|---|
| **Haki and Hekja** go inland and return with evidence of what they found (*Eiríks saga rauða*). | A concrete **Scout**. The decision is how far to send them and how long the company can afford to wait. *(Fieldcraft removed 2026-11-23; home OPEN.)* |
| **Gunnarr** gains access and information disguised as a pedlar (*Njáls saga*). | **Infiltrator** — was a Fieldcraft candidate (removed 2026-11-23; home OPEN). A famous fighter can also have a consequential utility role. |
| **Óláfr** uses the Irish taught by his mother Melkorka during a contested landing (*Laxdæla saga*). The burning in *Njáls saga* shows terms granted to particular people while a conflict continues. | Interpreter and envoy informed **Parley**, classified as a skill. |

| Ship reconstruction and saga scenes supply a **steersman, lookout, and boatman handling lines**. | Useful actions for particular maps. No Seamanship utility tree. |
