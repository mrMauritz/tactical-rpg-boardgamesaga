# 95 Historical Combat & Saga Realism (WIP)

This document tracks historical and Saga-based realities of small-scale medieval combat. These points serve as the conceptual basis for Layer A Design Atoms, countering faulty generic beliefs popularized by modern RPGs.

---

## Category Framework

These realities sort into six categories that organize the eventual Design Atom work:

| Category | Focus | Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **A. Equipment Reality** | Properties, limitations, and use of equipment | Shields, weapons, armor, degradation, reach, cloaks |
| **B. Human Physical Reality** | Physical capabilities and limitations of the human body | Fatigue, footing, grappling, wounds, stamina, senses |
| **C. Spatial Reality** | Position and environment determine possibilities | Terrain, distance, formations, flanking, numbers, indoors |
| **D. Psychological Reality** | Mental state influences combat | Fear, morale, taunts, feuds, champions, berserker state |
| **E. Combat Process** | Temporal flow of combat | Preparation → engagement → pauses → rout → aftermath |
| **F. Social Reality** | Cultural and societal factors surrounding combat | Weregild, ransom, looting, trophies, purification, scavenging |

### Category B — Development Order & Mechanical Assignment
Development begins with Category B. Each sub-topic resolves into an existing or newly-defined system rather than a standalone stat:

- **Fatigue** — Affects **SP Recovery** (and potentially starting SP) within the existing SP economy (Chapter 5, `05_battle_core.md`).
- **Footing** — Folded into the wider **Critical Fail** design space; recoverable via Skill/Ability saves rather than a standalone slip mechanic.
- **Grappling** — Delivered through **Progression Tree abilities**; requires a weapon or item carrying the appropriate **Tag** to activate.
- **Wounds** — Already treated as a serious consequence, escalated further by **Poison**. Emphasizes the importance of physicians and magical healers. *Reminder: opening a new Class Tree is costly, but grants some free starting skills.*
- **Stamina** — Fully covered by the **SP system**; SP economy management only grows more important as the system matures.
- **Senses** — Expanded below. Historical variables: **Vision, Hearing, Peripheral Awareness, Recognition, Attention, Detection.**

### Senses — Mechanical Resolution
| Variable | Resolution |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Vision** | New **Obscured** condition (fog, smoke, darkness, sun-glare, full helm) strips Active Defense eligibility against unseen attackers — see Chapter 7, `05_battle_core.md`. |
| **Hearing** | New **Deafened** condition (full helm, out of shout range) removes **Anticipated Defense** eligibility against Ambush/Surprise attacks — see Chapter 7, `05_battle_core.md`. |
| **Peripheral Awareness** | Already fully resolved by the existing **Facing** rules (Front/Flank/Rear, Chapter 7). No new mechanic — the entries below are the historical grounding for a rule that already exists. |
| **Recognition** | **Excluded from core systemization.** Treated strictly as a narrative special-case scenario (disguises, mixed-faction chaos), not a standalone mechanic. |
| **Attention** | Resolved as the **Engagement Cap** — see Chapter 5.1, `05_battle_core.md`, and the **Twin Ward** Perk (`02_perks_and_traits.md`, The Anchor). |
| **Detection** | Resolved as a **symmetric opposed check** (Stealth vs. Navigation & Awareness), usable identically by players and enemies — see Chapter 12, `06_adventure_and_prep.md`, and `03_skill_system.md`. The Hunt tree is the intended mastery path. |

## 1. The Shield is a Sacrificial, Consumable Tool
*   **The Myth:** A shield is a permanent gear item that provides a passive armor bonus.
*   **The Reality:** Shields were made of softwoods (linden, fir) designed to bind the enemy’s blade. They were rapidly hacked to pieces. In *Holmgang* (formal duels), rules explicitly allowed each combatant up to three shields because they expected them to be destroyed. A warrior without a backup shield was suddenly in extreme danger.

## 2. Melee Inevitably Devolves into Grappling (*Glíma*)
*   **The Myth:** Melee combatants stand one hex apart, taking turns swinging weapons until one HP bar empties.
*   **The Reality:** The moment combatants closed distance, weapon reach became a liability. Historical combat was incredibly grab-heavy. Sagas are filled with warriors drop-tackling opponents, grabbing weapon wrists, tripping, and using *Glíma* (folk wrestling) to pin an enemy to the dirt so they could finish them with a knife (*sax*) or a heavy stone.

## 3. Footing is More Lethal than Weapon Damage
*   **The Myth:** Terrain is just a static modifier (e.g., "-1 movement in mud").
*   **The Reality:** Slipping was a primary cause of death. Sagas constantly detail fights on wet river stones, seaweed-covered beaches, ice, or blood-slicked grass. In *Egil's Saga*, battles are won or lost because a warrior lost their footing on slippery ground, allowing an opponent to strike them while they struggled to regain balance.

## 4. Swords are Sidearms, Spears are Kings
*   **The Myth:** The sword is the primary, most powerful battlefield weapon.
*   **The Reality:** The spear (*spjót*) or hewing-spear (*kesja*) was the undisputed king of the battlefield due to leverage and reach. Swords were expensive status symbols, difficult to manufacture, and functionally acted as sidearms (like a modern pistol)—drawn only when a spear broke or the fight closed to grappling range.

## 5. Weapon Degradation Mid-Combat
*   **The Myth:** Weapons are indestructible unless subjected to a specific "degrade" spell.
*   **The Reality:** Iron was soft and prone to bending. The Sagas frequently describe warriors mid-skirmish having to step on their swords to straighten them out after hitting a shield or helmet. Axes chipped on iron rims, and spear shafts were regularly shorn in half. Fighting required managing the physical structural limits of your tools.

## 6. Armor is Impervious to Slashes (Targeting Gaps)
*   **The Myth:** Wearing mail simply reduces incoming slash damage by a flat percentage.
*   **The Reality:** You cannot cut through iron mail with a sword swing. A slash against mail did zero cutting damage (though it could cause blunt trauma/broken bones). To kill an armored opponent, warriors had to thrust specifically into gaps: the throat, the groin, under the armpits, or through the eye-slits.

## 7. Combat Pauses and Mutually Agreed Rests
*   **The Myth:** Combat is an uninterrupted, high-octane exchange of actions until one side is dead.
*   **The Reality:** Swinging a 4-pound weapon and wrestling in wool and iron is exhaustingly heavy. Sagas note that in prolonged duels, combatants would mutually agree to pause, sit down, catch their breath, drink, and clean the blood off their hands before resuming the fight.

## 8. The Danger of Wide Swings in Formations
*   **The Myth:** You can swing a massive two-handed axe or greatsword while standing shoulder-to-shoulder with allies without consequence.
*   **The Reality:** A Dane axe requires space. Swinging one in a tight shield wall (*skjaldborg*) would decapitate your own allies. Formations restricted weapon usage; inside the wall, warriors were limited to thrusts (spears and seaxes). To use a heavy chopping weapon, you had to step out of the formation, exposing yourself to throw off the safety of the line.

## 9. Minor Wounds Kill Later (Fever and Infection)
*   **The Myth:** If you survive a battle with 1 HP, you can rest at an inn and wake up at 100%.
*   **The Reality:** In the sagas, many warriors "won" their fights only to die three days later in their beds. A shallow cut from a dirty iron blade or an arrow tip regularly led to gangrene, lockjaw, or systemic fever. Surviving the battlefield was only the first check; surviving the aftermath was the second.

## 10. Battles End at the "Breaking Point," Not 0 HP
*   **The Myth:** Enemies fight to the last man with fanatical devotion.
*   **The Reality:** Medieval skirmishes were fought by people who wanted to go home to their farms. Once the local chieftain or the strongest champion was cut down, the remaining fighters instantly routed, surrendered, or offered monetary compensation (*weregild*) to settle the feud. Fighting to the death was rare and reserved for those facing absolute execution.

## 11. Throwing Weapons Mid-Charge
*   **The Myth:** Ranged combat is for archers standing in the backline; melee fighters only use hand-to-hand weapons.
*   **The Reality:** Warriors almost always carried throwing spears, light axes, or even heavy rocks to hurl mid-charge. This was done right before the lines collided to force the enemy to raise or drop their shields, creating gaps for the follow-up melee strike.

## 12. The "Single Leg" Target and Below-the-Knee Strikes
*   **The Myth:** Attacks target the abstract "body" of the defender, or headshots are the ultimate goal.
*   **The Reality:** Because shields naturally covered the head and torso, the legs were the most exposed targets. Sagas contain countless descriptions of warriors striking low to sever a foot or knee. A leg wound was rarely immediately fatal, but it instantly ended the defender's ability to stand, fight, or flee.

## 13. Combat is Loud and Highly Verbal (Taunts and Verse)
*   **The Myth:** Combat is fought in silent, focused concentration.
*   **The Reality:** Psychological warfare was a mechanical reality. Warriors constantly shouted insults, bragged, chanted poetry, and mocked their enemies' lineage mid-fight to break their resolve. A well-timed insult could provoke an enemy into a reckless, fatal mistake.

## 14. Feuds and Personal Target Selection (The Blood Debt)
*   **The Myth:** Soldiers target the nearest threat in the name of tactical efficiency.
*   **The Reality:** Skirmishes were highly personal. If an enemy had killed your brother, you would ignore closer targets and charge straight for them to settle the blood debt. Combat was driven by emotional targets, often overriding optimal tactical play.

## 15. The Use of Cloaks as Improvisational Off-Hand Shields
*   **The Myth:** Without a shield or dual-wielding weapon, the off-hand is useless.
*   **The Reality:** If caught without a shield, warriors wrapped their heavy woolen cloaks around their off-arm to catch or redirect blade strikes. Cloaks were also thrown over enemy weapons or faces to temporarily blind them or bind their guard.

## 16. Armor restricts Stamina (Cardio), Not Movement Speed
*   **The Myth:** Heavily armored combatants move in slow motion and cannot jump or sprint.
*   **The Reality:** A well-fitted suit of mail distributes its weight across the shoulders and hips. A warrior could sprint, vault obstacles, and roll. However, the weight and heat retention placed a massive tax on the wearer's lungs and heart, draining stamina much faster.

## 17. The Lethal Reality of "Indoor" Feuds (House Burning)
*   **The Myth:** Skirmishes take place on open, fair tactical grids.
*   **The Reality:** Many saga fights occurred at homesteads. Rather than fighting a defender room-by-room, attackers frequently resorted to *hús-brenna* (house-burning)—barricading the doors and setting the roof on fire to burn or suffocate the occupants inside. 

## 18. Unorthodox Blow Angles (Hooking and Underswings)
*   **The Myth:** Attacks are simple thrusts or downward slashes.
*   **The Reality:** Experienced fighters used the geometry of their weapons creatively. Axe-wielders used the beard of the axe to hook the top of an enemy's shield and pull it down, exposing their throat for an ally's spear. Swordsmen swung from low, sweeping angles to bypass the bottom edge of shields.

## 19. Numerical Advantage is Exponentially Brutal
*   **The Myth:** A skilled master can easily parry and defeat three or four average attackers simultaneously.
*   **The Reality:** In real life, being outnumbered 2-to-1 or 3-to-1 is almost always a death sentence. You cannot split your focus to cover two separate angles of attack at once; while you parry the front strike, the second attacker hits your flank or rear.

## 20. Self-Treatment and Brutal Field Triage
*   **The Myth:** Wounds are treated after combat with magical potions or bandages that immediately restore vitality.
*   **The Reality:** Warriors performed immediate, grim diagnoses on themselves. Sagas describe injured men eating onions to see if a stomach wound smelled of the vegetable (which indicated a punctured bowel and certain death) or calmly pulling arrowheads out of their own chests to see how deep they went.

## 21. Pre-Battle Champions and Single Combat (Sengoku Japan / Islamic Conquests / Europe)
*   **The Myth:** Armies clash immediately in a massive, anonymous head-on melee.
*   **The Reality:** Small-scale combat and larger battles often began with champions stepping forward. In Sengoku Japan (*Kaburaya* phase) and early Islamic conquests (*Mubarizun*), champions shouted their lineage, achievements, and challenges. Winning or losing these opening duels directly determined the morale and combat willingness (SP/Will) of the entire force before any rank-and-file clashing occurred.

## 22. Pre-Combat Signaling and Psychological Ambush (Mesoamerica / North America)
*   **The Myth:** Ambushes are silent until the moment a trap is sprung.
*   **The Reality:** Indigenous American skirmish tactics relied on pre-combat sensory overload. Warriors used animal-mimicking whistles, clay whistles (like the Aztec death whistle), and coordinated war cries to create a terrifying wall of sound just before striking. The goal was to cause cognitive freeze and panic, disrupting defensive cohesion before physical contact was made.

## 23. The Labor of Gear Preparation and Ritual Purification (Global)
*   **The Myth:** Warriors slip into armor instantly and sit around camp waiting for the battle to start.
*   **The Reality:** Putting on mail or plate was an exhausting ritual that required assistants (squires/retinue). Pre-combat hours were spent oiling leather straps, checking rivets, writing death poems (Japan), or performing religious purification rituals. Armor was hot, heavy, and claustrophobic; putting it on too early meant dehydrating before the fight even began.

## 24. Pre-Combat Stimulants and Ritual Altered States (Siberia / Americas / Nordics)
*   **The Myth:** Warriors enter combat with a clear, sober mind.
*   **The Reality:** To bypass the body's natural self-preservation mechanisms, many cultures used pre-combat stimulants. Siberian warriors used fly agaric mushrooms, Mesoamericans consumed pulque or sacred herbs, and Norse *berserkir* used alcohol or plant extracts to induce a state of physical analgesia (numbness to pain) and high adrenaline.

## 25. Micro-Tactical Weather and Sun Alignment (HEMA / China / Sengoku Japan)
*   **The Myth:** Weather and time of day are just cosmetic backdrops to a fight.
*   **The Reality:** Experienced tacticians would delay engagements specifically to position themselves with the sun or the wind at their backs. Blinding the enemy with morning glare or throwing dust/rain into their eyes was a primary pre-combat objective. Rain was particularly lethal to archers (ruining bowstrings) and firearms, shifting tactical value instantly to heavy melee.

## 26. Blunt Trauma and Pommel Striking Against Plate Armor (HEMA / Europe)
*   **The Myth:** Swords are used exclusively for cutting and slicing.
*   **The Reality:** Swords are useless for cutting steel armor. European HEMA manuals detail the *mordhau* (murder stroke), where a warrior grabs their sword by the blade with gloved hands and uses the heavy pommel or guard as a hammer to concuss and crack the helmet of an armored opponent.

## 27. Half-Swording and Grappling in Armor (Europe / Japan)
*   **The Myth:** Armored combat is a series of wide, clean sword swings.
*   **The Reality:** Combat between heavily armored opponents (such as European knights or samurai in Yoroi) resembled violent wrestling matches. Warriors used "half-swording"—gripping the middle of their own blade to turn the sword into a short, stiff lever to force the point into armor gaps (gaskets, visors) while attempting to throw or trip the opponent.

## 28. Ranged Weapons as Disruption and Control (Mongol / Nomadic)
*   **The Myth:** Ranged weapons are used primarily for sniping high-value targets.
*   **The Reality:** Nomadic archers fired massed volleys not expecting single-shot kills, but to disrupt enemy movement and force them to raise shields. Firing at the mounts of cavalry or the legs of infantry created physical obstructions, rendering the enemy vulnerable to a decisive charge.

## 29. Reach and Spatial Zone Control (Global / Spear Tactics)
*   **The Myth:** A swordsman can easily close the distance on a spearman.
*   **The Reality:** Bypassing a spear tip is incredibly difficult. A spearman controls a large physical "zone of threat." If the spearman manages their distance correctly, the swordsman is mathematically unable to land a hit without first successfully binding or displacing the spear shaft, which is a high-risk action.

## 30. Dual Wielding is Defensive/Offensive Division (Japan / HEMA)
*   **The Myth:** Dual wielding allows a fighter to execute rapid, double-handed hacking combos.
*   **The Reality:** In historical dual-weapon styles (like Miyamoto Musashi's *Niten Ichi-ryu* or European rapier and dagger), the off-hand weapon or tool (dagger, buckler, or short sword) was used almost exclusively to parry, bind, or cover the enemy's weapon. The primary weapon was then used to strike. You did not swing both simultaneously, as it left you completely unprotected.

## 31. Edge-on-Edge Parries Destroy Swords (HEMA / Kendo)
*   **The Myth:** Defending involves blocking the incoming blade perpendicular to your own blade's edge.
*   **The Reality:** Perpendicular edge-on-edge blocks create deep notches that ruin the heat-treatment and structural integrity of swords, causing them to shatter. Defending relied on "slipping" or redirecting the blow using the flat of the blade or the strong part (*forte*) near the hilt, allowing the attacker's momentum to glide off harmlessly.

## 32. Sensory Deprivation of Helmed Warriors (Global)
*   **The Myth:** Helmets offer head protection with no trade-offs.
*   **The Reality:** Wearing a fully enclosed helmet (like a European great helm or a samurai *kabuto*) severely restricted vision and hearing. peripheral vision was practically non-existent, and breathing was highly restricted. helmed warriors were incredibly vulnerable to flank attacks simply because they could not see or hear them coming.

## 33. Armor is Useless Without Padding (Global)
*   **The Myth:** Wearing chainmail directly over clothing provides full protection.
*   **The Reality:** Mail stops cuts, but does nothing to absorb kinetic impact. A blow from an axe or mace over bare mail would easily drive the iron rings into the flesh and shatter the bones beneath. Armor required a thick, energy-absorbing padded garment underneath (such as a gambeson or *hitatare*) to be functional.

## 34. Complete Pragmatism and Dirty Tactics (Koryu / HEMA)
*   **The Myth:** Historical martial arts were built on elegant, noble, and honorable dueling codes.
*   **The Reality:** Survival manuals (like Fiore dei Liberi's works or Japanese Koryu scrolls) explicitly teach dirty tactics as standard solutions. Warriors bit, gouged eyes, grabbed throats, kicked groins, threw dirt in faces, and used their weapons' crossguards to choke or hook their opponents.

## 35. Post-Combat Trophy Taking and Decapitation (Sengoku Japan / Celts)
*   **The Myth:** Once the battle ends, warriors immediately tend to their wounded or rest.
*   **The Reality:** In many cultures, the immediate post-battle action was securing proof of kills. Sengoku samurai would decapitate fallen enemies and carry their heads (*kubi*) to present to their commanders for official rewards. This created a high-risk post-combat window where warriors were distracted by looting and harvesting trophies, leaving them vulnerable to counter-ambushes.

## 36. Scavenging and Field Gear Salvage (Global)
*   **The Myth:** Dead bodies and discarded gear are left to rot on the battlefield.
*   **The Reality:** Iron, leather, and weapons were incredibly valuable commodities. Immediately after a battle, the victors spent hours stripping corpses of their armor, boots, and weapons. Scavengers and blacksmiths salvaged damaged mail rings, broken spearheads, and bent swords for recycling and repair.

## 37. Capturing for Ransom vs. Battlefield Execution (Europe / Mesoamerica)
*   **The Myth:** The objective in small-scale combat is always to slaughter every opponent.
*   **The Reality:** Wealthy knights and nobles in Europe were far more valuable alive than dead because they could be ransomed for fortunes. In Mesoamerican *Flower Wars*, the mechanical objective was to capture opponents unharmed for ritual sacrifice. Killing an opponent in battle was seen as a failure of skill; combat was tuned to disable, disarm, and capture.

## 38. The Post-Combat Crash and Blood-Madness Aftermath (Norse / Japan)
*   **The Myth:** Combatants return to normal emotional states the moment the fight stops.
*   **The Reality:** The massive adrenaline dump of combat left survivors physically and mentally shattered. The Sagas describe *berserksgangr* aftermath as a state of extreme physical weakness, shivering, and deep depression. In Japan, returning warriors had to undergo purification rites to cleanse the spiritual pollution (*kegare*) of having killed.

## 39. Non-Combat Deaths from Armor Wear (Global)
*   **The Myth:** Armor can be worn all day with no side effects.
*   **The Reality:** The weight, tight strapping, and heat retention of full armor worn for hours caused severe chafing, heat exhaustion, and dehydration. In hot climates, warriors frequently collapsed and died of heatstroke without ever receiving a physical blow from the enemy.

## 40. Post-Victory Break of Discipline and Looting Vulnerability (Global)
*   **The Myth:** A victorious army maintains formation and marches home in order.
*   **The Reality:** The moment a routing enemy broke, the victorious warriors' discipline collapsed as they scrambled to loot the enemy's baggage train or strip dead bodies. Historically, many battles were turned around and won by the "defeated" side because they rallied while the victors were disorganized and busy plundering.

## 41. Vision Is a Hard Gate, Not a Soft Modifier (Global / Norse)
*   **The Myth:** Reduced visibility is a cosmetic accuracy penalty layered on top of an otherwise all-seeing combatant.
*   **The Reality:** Fog, night, and smoke didn't shave points off accuracy — they made combatants functionally blind to anything outside a few feet. At the 1471 Battle of Barnet, dense fog caused the Earl of Oxford's own returning troops to be mistaken for the enemy and attacked by their allies. Norse raiders deliberately struck at dawn mist or used the smoke of a burning hall (*hús-brenna*) to mask their approach and escape. In these conditions, "seeing" your attacker at all was the precondition for defending yourself — not a bonus to a roll.

## 42. Hearing Is the First Warning System (Global / Norse)
*   **The Myth:** Combat awareness is purely visual; sound is atmospheric flavor.
*   **The Reality:** Watchmen and household dogs existed because sound reached further and faster than sight. Norse settlements relied on horn-blasts (*lur*) to raise the alarm long before an enemy was visible, and sagas describe defenders arming themselves the moment they heard raised voices or hoofbeats outside — often the only warning before a night attack. On the field, war-horns and drums carried commands through smoke and dust when hand signals and shouted orders were physically invisible or drowned out.

## 43. Peripheral Blindness Under Combat Stress (Global / Modern Combat Science)
*   **The Myth:** A skilled fighter maintains full 360-degree situational awareness for the whole engagement.
*   **The Reality:** Acute stress narrows both vision (tunnel vision) and hearing (auditory exclusion) — a well-documented physiological response, not a failure of training. Sagas are full of warriors locked in a duel who never see the third party who ends the fight; single combats were frequently interrupted by an oath-bound kinsman or a bystander settling an unrelated score, exploiting exactly this narrowed focus.

## 44. Divided Attention Kills Faster Than Divided Numbers (Global / HEMA)
*   **The Myth:** A master swordsman can track and respond to several attackers with equal precision, as if fighting each one in isolation.
*   **The Reality:** Historical fencing manuals (Fiore dei Liberi, Ringeck) explicitly teach that a lone fighter facing multiple opponents must maneuver to keep one enemy physically between himself and the rest at all times — because human attention cannot truly split. The moment two attackers gain simultaneous separate angles, the manuals treat the fight as effectively lost without terrain or luck intervening. This reinforces Entry 19: numerical disadvantage is not a matter of stacked accuracy penalties, it is a matter of attention running out.

## 45. Detection Runs Both Ways (Global / Norse)
*   **The Myth:** Ambushes are a one-directional storytelling device sprung on the party, never a tool the party wields first.
*   **The Reality:** Scouting and counter-scouting (*njósn*) were a central, mutual pre-battle skill in the sagas — parties sent riders ahead specifically to detect an enemy's ambush before walking into it, and canny defenders posted their own scouts to detect the scouts. Sun Tzu codifies the same principle: foreknowledge decides the engagement before either side draws a weapon. Detection was never a power unique to the side springing the trap; it was a contest, and whoever won it first controlled the terms of the fight.
