## 1. Definition

**ESTABLISHED:** "Divinity" is broader than gods — it can include gods, spirits, ancestors, sacred beings, sacred places, forces, and other concrete powers of the spiritual world. Divinities share the vessel structure of Companions (`21_COMPANIONS_WIP.md`) and Factions (`22_FACTIONS_WIP.md`) but operate on a mythic scale:

- Companions carry personal history and individual power.
- Factions carry collective history and worldly power.
- Divinities carry mythic history and spiritual power.

Divine movement is less legible than factional movement: apparent change may mean the divinity changed, a different aspect appeared, worshippers misunderstood it, the relationship changed, or apparently separate manifestations were connected. Factions shape historical direction; divinities shape the spiritual possibility-horizon — what may become sacred, cursed, meaningful, forbidden, or spiritually possible.

## 2. Candidate Roster (Not Locked)

Five Old Norse divine-bond candidates were raised for discussion:

- **Odin** — knowledge, sacrifice, runes, sorcery, hidden victory.
- **Thor** — strength, protection, direct action, opposition to destructive powers.
- **Freyja** — seiðr, desire, fertility, beauty, relation to the dead.
- **Týr** — oath, law, courage, sacrifice for order.
- **Eir** — healing and preservation.

**Status:** the request for five Norse gods is ESTABLISHED; the exact five and their final trees are OPEN.

## 3. Relationship to Bonds and Oaths

Divinity bonds are one of the Bond families (`24_BONDS_AND_DEVOTION_WIP.md` §2.2) — magic, healing, protection, and other supernatural capabilities arise from a particular relationship, not one abstract universal pool. A divine oath's consequences (favour, prohibition, curse, sign) are distinct from a faction oath's — see `96_OATHS_WIP.md` §12 "Two Worlds of Oaths."
